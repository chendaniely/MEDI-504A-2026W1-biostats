Cleaning and tidying medical data

Before you can model data, it has to be tidy and clean: one row per observation, one column per variable, and values coded the way you’ll analyze them. In class, we’ll do one exercise that reshapes data into a tidy shape, and one that recodes numbers into categories we can read. For more practice, see Extra practice: cleaning and tidying.

library(tidyverse)
library(medicaldata)

Exercise 1: Gum health over the trial’s visits

opt (from medicaldata) is the Obstetrics and Periodontal Therapy trial: pregnant women with periodontal disease were randomized to periodontal treatment (T) or control (C) (Michalowicz et al., 2006). Their gums were examined at baseline (BL), visit 3 (V3), and visit 5 (V5). We’ll look at two of the measures: the gingival index (GE, where higher means more inflamed gums) and the average pocket depth in millimetres (PD.avg).
